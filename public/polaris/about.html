National Centre for Polar & Ocean Research (NCPOR)

Established under the Ministry of Earth Sciences, Government of India, NCPOR serves as the nodal agency for the planning, coordination, and execution of India's polar expeditions to Antarctica, the Arctic, and the Southern Ocean. India maintains a continuous, uninterrupted scientific presence in Antarctica since 1983.

44+ Expeditions Completed
Mandate

Polar & Cryosphere Sciences

Executing high-impact multidisciplinary scientific research in glaciology, ice-core paleoclimatology, polar biology, atmospheric trace gases, and global climate feedback mechanisms.

Operations

Year-Round Remote Stations

Safely sustaining permanent human habitation and automated observatories at Maitri (Schirmacher Oasis) and Bharati (Larsemann Hills) in extreme polar weather conditions.

Governance

Antarctic Treaty Compliance

Strictly upholding the Antarctic Treaty System and the Protocol on Environmental Protection (Madrid Protocol), ensuring zero environmental footprint and peaceful scientific cooperation.

Historic Milestones & Station Chronology

Four decades of Indian scientific excellence on the frozen continent
1981

Operation Gangotri

First Indian Scientific Expedition to Antarctica led by Dr. S. Z. Qasim landed on Queen Maud Land on 9 January 1982.

1983

Dakshin Gangotri

India's first permanent research station constructed on the ice shelf. Transitioned to an unmanned automated observatory in 1990.

1989

Maitri Station

Inland permanent research station built on ice-free rocky terrain in Schirmacher Oasis with water pipeline to Priyadarshini Lake.

2012

Bharati Station

State-of-the-art aerodynamic modular station in Larsemann Hills, featuring high-speed satellite telemetry and cogeneration power.

2026+

Maitri-II Project

Next-generation green polar research station featuring zero-emission solar-wind microgrid and automated cryogenic laboratories.
